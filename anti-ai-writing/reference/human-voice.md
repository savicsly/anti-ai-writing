# Human Voice & Persona Style Guides

How to make writing sound like a person with a point of view — not a template. Use this file when the draft is correct but personality-free.

---

## Conversational writing

### How humans actually speak on the page

- Contractions (`don't`, `we're`, `it's`)
- Direct address when earned (`you`)
- Occasional fragments
- Specific nouns over abstract categories
- Opinions owned with `I` when appropriate

### Natural interruptions

Humans course-correct:

- "Wait — that's not quite right."
- "Or rather…"
- Parenthetical asides (the kind you'd say under your breath)

AI tends to smooth these out. Reintroduce a few when the genre allows.

### Imperfect sentences

Not errors for their own sake — texture:

- Starting with `And` or `But`
- A short line after a long one
- Leaving a thought slightly open

### Varying rhythm

Meaning drives length. Punch lines go short. Explanations can run. Avoid mechanical long–short alternation.

### Storytelling techniques

- Time and place stamps ("Tuesday at midnight")
- Named people and objects
- Sensory detail when emotion matters
- Show the awkward moment; don't announce "I felt joy"

### Voice and personality

Voice is repeated preference: words you reach for, jokes you'd make, hills you'll die on. AI averages these away. Feed the model (or the edit pass) with the author's real tells.

## What to add back

| Missing human element | How to restore it |
|-----------------------|-------------------|
| Specificity | Replace "many companies" with one named example |
| Stance | Cut "arguably"; say what you think |
| Texture | One fragment, one aside, one uneven paragraph |
| Memory | A detail only this writer would know |
| Risk | A claim that could be disagreed with |

## Style guides by persona

These are targets for voice — not costumes. Stay accurate; change register.

### Write like a journalist

- Lead with the news
- Attribution over vibe
- Short paragraphs
- Plain verbs
- Cut throat-clearing and moralizing endings

**Avoid:** "In a world where…", unnamed "experts say", inspirational kicker.

### Write like a founder

- Concrete numbers and tradeoffs
- First person plural when it's the team
- Direct about risk and constraints
- Little patience for buzzwords

**Avoid:** pitch-deck poetry, "revolutionize", fake customer quotes.

### Write like an engineer

- Precise terms; define once
- Prefer "what breaks" over "benefits"
- Examples from systems, not metaphors
- Active voice for actions; passive only when the actor is irrelevant

**Avoid:** "seamlessly", "robust solution", anthropomorphizing the stack.

### Write like a professor

- Claim → evidence → implication
- Cite specifics; name researchers
- Keep disciplinary conventions (including passive voice where expected)
- Still vary sentence length — formal ≠ metronomic

**Avoid:** "This paper aims to explore", "a plethora of studies", "fill a gap in the literature" as empty ritual.

### Write like a marketer

- One promise, proof, ask
- Benefits tied to observable outcomes
- Respect the reader's time

**Avoid:** adjective stacks, "unlock", "elevate your brand", fake urgency.

### Write like a creator

- Talk to one person
- Cold opens welcome
- Personality over polish
- CTAs that sound like a real ask

**Avoid:** "Hey guys welcome back", "smash subscribe", scripted awe.

### Write like a friend

- Short
- Assume shared context
- Fragments fine
- No formal greeting/closing theater

**Avoid:** full essays in DMs; "I hope you're doing well!"; numbered points in a text.

## Quick voice audit

Read the draft aloud and ask:

1. Would a specific person I know say this out loud?
2. Can I point to one detail that isn't generic?
3. Is there a sentence I'd actually fight to keep?
4. Did I remove every interesting rough edge?

If (1)–(3) are no and (4) is yes, rewrite for voice before shipping.
