# AI Introductions & Conclusions

Formulaic openings and closings are among the strongest structural fingerprints of LLM prose. Use this file when a draft starts too broadly or ends with a moralizing wrap-up.

---

## Why openings sound fake

AI intros often:

- Start with a temporal landscape ("In today's…")
- Define the topic before saying anything useful
- Promise what "this article will explore"
- Avoid first person, mistakes, and concrete scenes
- Use rhetorical-question hooks ("Have you ever wondered…?")

Bloomberry's **Generic Opener** shape: `[Temporal frame], [claim/lead-in]`.

---

## AI introductions by format

### Blog articles

**Common AI openings:**

- "Technology is changing the world."
- "In today's digital age…"
- "Artificial intelligence has transformed…"
- "Whether you're a beginner or a pro…"
- "In this article, we will explore…"

**Why fake:** abstract setup; delayed point; SEO throat-clearing.

**Human alternatives:**

- Start with the actual claim
- Open on a specific incident, number, or failure
- Put the reader in a scene, then zoom out if needed

**AI:** "Artificial intelligence (AI) is revolutionizing industries worldwide. In this article, we will explore the key aspects of AI integration."

**Human:** "Last Tuesday our main database crashed at midnight. A 40-line script fixed it — no ML involved. That made me ask how we're actually using AI day to day."

### LinkedIn openings

**Common AI openings:**

- "Yesterday I learned a valuable lesson about leadership."
- "I used to think X. Then Y happened."
- "Here's what nobody tells you about…"
- "3 lessons from…"
- "Hot take:"

**Why fake:** vulnerability template; lesson pre-announced; engagement bait.

**Human alternatives:**

- Name the specific person, company, or moment
- Share the awkward detail, not the polished moral
- Skip "I learned" — show the change

### YouTube openings

**Common AI openings:**

- "Hey everyone, welcome back to the channel!"
- "In today's video we're going to talk about…"
- "Before we begin, make sure you…"
- "Have you ever…?"
- "Let's dive right in" / "Without further ado"

**Why fake:** ritual greeting + agenda dump; retention clichés.

**Human alternatives:**

- Cold open on the most interesting second of the video
- One-line promise, then start doing the thing
- Talk to one person, not "everyone"

### Newsletter openings

**Common AI openings:**

- "Hope you're having a great week!"
- "Quick one today…"
- "Here's what's inside this issue…"
- "As I was reflecting on…"

**Why fake:** calendar small-talk before news.

**Human alternatives:**

- Lead with the item that earned the send
- Keep the "what's inside" list only if the issue is long

### Essay / academic openings

**Common AI openings:**

- "In recent years, there has been growing interest in…"
- "This paper aims to explore…"
- "Since the dawn of…"
- Broad historical sweep before the thesis

**Why fake:** throat-clearing; vague stakes.

**Human alternatives:**

- Thesis early
- Specific puzzle, dataset, or contradiction
- Cite a real claim, not "many researchers"

## AI conclusions

AI endings tend to restate the thesis with grand language or a tidy moral. Bloomberry's **Resolution Closer** wraps forward-looking and neat: "The path forward is clear…"

### Types of weak endings

| Type | Typical cues | Fix |
|------|--------------|-----|
| Summary ending | In conclusion, To summarize, Overall | Delete cue; stop when done |
| Moralizing ending | We must remember that… | Replace with a concrete next step |
| Inspirational ending | The future belongs to those who… | End on a specific decision |
| CTA ending | Don't forget to like/subscribe / Take action today | One clear ask, in plain language |
| Key-takeaway ending | A key takeaway is… | Just state the takeaway |
| Underscoring ending | This underscores the importance of… | Name the implication once |

**AI:** "In conclusion, effective communication is essential in the modern workplace."

**Human:** "Communicating well actually makes the work easier — I learned that the hard way."

### Human replacements

- End on one useful thought
- End on a question the reader can act on
- End mid-energy (especially blogs) — no bow needed
- For essays: implications + limits, not a sermon
- For emails: the ask, then stop

Not every piece needs a formal conclusion.

---

## Open / close pairs to avoid

| Opening habit | Matching closing habit |
|---------------|------------------------|
| In today's landscape… | Ultimately, the path forward is clear… |
| In this article we will explore… | In conclusion, we have seen that… |
| Have you ever wondered…? | So next time you… remember… |
| Let's dive in… | And there you have it… |
| I used to believe X… | The key takeaway is Y… |

If both ends of the piece use stock frames, rewrite both.

---

## Editing checklist

- [ ] First sentence carries a real claim, scene, or fact
- [ ] No "In today's / In this article / Let's dive" opener unless authentic to the voice
- [ ] Ending has no "In conclusion / Ultimately / Key takeaway" scaffolding
- [ ] Closing does not merely restate the introduction
- [ ] Genre-appropriate: essays may need implications; social posts may end abruptly
